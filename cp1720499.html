Meet the Board

Your elected volunteer directors of Condominium Corporation No. 1720499. The Board makes decisions on policies, the budget, capital projects, and bylaw enforcement on behalf of all owners.

How the Board works: Directors are owners elected at the Annual General Meeting. They serve without pay, meet regularly throughout the year, and rely on Riverdale Property Management to handle day-to-day operations. For most issues, please contact the Manager first — the Manager will escalate to the Board when needed.

📅 Annual General Meeting (AGM): Every owner is invited. This is where directors are elected, the budget is approved, and major decisions are discussed. Watch your email and the front lobby for the notice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Plain-language answers to the questions homeowners ask most often.

Where can I park my vehicle, and where can guests park?

Each owner has designated parking spaces shown on the Corporation's parking plan. Vehicles must stay within painted lines — you can't park behind your stall or in front of garages. Visitors must use marked visitor parking stalls only, available first-come, first-served.

If you (the owner) need to use visitor parking for more than 72 hours, you must notify the Condominium Manager. Parking in fire lanes (behind/between garage doors and on roadways) is strictly prohibited — vehicles may be towed.

See: Policy 1 · Bylaw 62(c)
Can I have a dog or cat? Are there size limits?

Yes — domesticated pets (cats, dogs, birds, fish, small animals) are allowed. Dogs must be 16 inches or less at the shoulder when fully grown. Certified service dogs are exempt with Board approval.

Not allowed: exotic, unusual, unknown, or venomous animals (unless specifically approved by the Board).

Pets must be hand-leashed at all times on common property. Owners must clean up after their pets immediately. Any damage to common property caused by your pet is charged back to you.

See: Policy 9 · Bylaw 62(b)(ii)
Do I need approval before renovating my unit?

Yes — for most work. Minor cosmetic changes (e.g., painting walls) do not need approval. Everything else does, including:

  • Any change to load-bearing walls, plumbing, mechanical, or electrical systems
  • Any structural, layout, or fixture renovation

Before starting, submit an accurate rendering of the proposed work to the Board for written approval. Use licensed contractors with proper permits. An engineer's report may be required for major work. Owners are fully responsible for any damage to common property, and all construction debris must be removed off-site — not placed in the household trash bins.

See: Policy 12 · Bylaw 3(d)
Can I rent out my unit? What are the requirements?

Yes, owners may lease their units, but you must:

  • Provide tenant contact information (including emergency contacts) to the Condominium Manager
  • Include a clause in the lease requiring the tenant to follow all Bylaws and Policies
  • Ensure tenants carry full contents insurance, additional living expense coverage, and up to $1 million general liability
  • As the landlord, you are responsible for relaying all relevant condo information to your tenant
See: Policy 11 · Bylaw 51
What insurance do I personally need?

The Corporation insures the building itself, but each owner must carry their own condominium insurance. Speak to your broker about:

  • Personal Property (contents) — minimum recommended $50,000 for fire, water, windstorm, etc.
  • Improvements & Betterments — upgrades made by you or previous owners are usually not covered by the Corporation
  • Additional Living Expenses — enough to live elsewhere for at least 18 months in case of a total loss
  • Loss Assessment Coverage — covers your share of any special assessment after an insured loss
See: Policy 10 · Bylaw 46
When are holiday lights allowed?

Holiday decorations may go up from November 1st each year and must come down by March 1st. Please turn lights off by February 1st. For other seasonal occasions (birthdays, cultural holidays, etc.), decorations are permitted up to 3 weeks before and must come down within 3 weeks after the occasion.

As a courtesy, please turn lights off by 11 p.m.

See: Policy 7
Can I install an air conditioner?

Yes, with conditions: installation must be approved by the Board in advance, comply with building codes, and be performed by a licensed contractor carrying WCB coverage and at least $1 million general liability insurance. Ongoing maintenance, servicing, and repair costs are the unit owner's responsibility.

See: Policy 8
What's the rule on noise?

Any noise that is a nuisance or unreasonably interferes with another resident's use and enjoyment of their unit or the common property is prohibited at any hour. Construction or renovation work is restricted to weekdays between 8:30 a.m. and 6:00 p.m. — work outside those hours, or on weekends and statutory holidays, requires Board consent.

See: Policy 2 · Bylaw 62(b)(vi)
What can I put on my balcony or patio?

Balconies and patios must be kept clean, tidy, and free of clutter, debris, garbage, or anything that could be a safety hazard. Hanging laundry or other items in a way that affects the building's appearance is not permitted.

See: Policy 5 · Bylaw 62(b)(viii)
Can I install a doorbell camera or security light?

If you wish to install any device — security lights, doorbell cameras, motion sensors, etc. — on communal property (bannisters, pillars, exteriors), you must get Board approval first.

See: Policy 3(b)
How does garbage and recycling work?

Use the designated areas and follow the City of Edmonton's waste management guidelines. Don't tamper with or remove items from waste containers. Large items (furniture, appliances) and hazardous materials must be disposed of off-site at the owner's expense — not left in common areas or trash bins.

See: Policy 4
What window coverings are allowed?

Window coverings must keep the exterior of the building looking uniform. Reflective coverings that could disturb neighbouring units' privacy are not allowed. Flags, bed sheets, aluminum foil or other improvised coverings visible from outside are not permitted.

See: Policy 6
How do I report a bylaw violation?

Document the incident in writing — include the date, time, location, nature of the offense, photos if you have them, and any witnesses. Submit it to the Condominium Manager (Nadeem Walji) by email or mail. The Manager will acknowledge receipt in writing and bring it to the Board's next meeting. See the full process.

See: Violation Reporting Process · Bylaw 43
What happens if I (or a tenant) break a rule?

The Board issues fines on an escalating schedule:

  • 1st offense: $50 + written warning
  • 2nd offense (within 6 months): $100 + formal notice of violation
  • 3rd offense (within 12 months): $200 + possible hearing
  • Repeated further offenses: $250 (the maximum allowed under the Bylaws)

Fines are payable within 10–14 days; interest may be charged on unpaid amounts. Owners have a right to appeal under the Alberta Condominium Property Act. See the full Fine Schedule.

See: Fine Schedule · Bylaw 43
Who do I contact for what?

Day-to-day questions, maintenance issues, violation reports → Nadeem Walji, Condominium Manager (Riverdale Property Management)

Board matters, governance, escalations → Nico Bautista, Board President

See the Contacts section for full details.

Policies (Full Text)

These policies are reviewed at least once per year. Monetary sanctions may be imposed under Bylaw 43.

Authority: Bylaws and Policies are established under the Alberta Condominium Property Act. Under Bylaw 3(e) & (h), owners must comply with all bylaws and rules and ensure their tenants, family, visitors, and other occupants do the same. Under Bylaw 5(j), the Board is authorized to make and enforce rules for the use, enjoyment, and safety of the Common Property.

1. Parking

  • Each owner is entitled to their designated parking space per the Corporation's parking plan. Vehicles must be parked within painted lines; additional vehicles cannot be parked behind stalls or in front of garages. (Bylaw 62(c)(i))
  • Parking units may only be used for parking vehicles. Recreational use (playing, ball games, biking, scootering) of parking spaces and roadways is not permitted. (Bylaw 62(c)(ii))
  • Owners' vehicles cannot be parked on common property. Parking in fire lanes (behind and between garage doors and all roadways) or other non-designated areas is strictly prohibited. (Bylaw 62(c)(iv))
  • Visitors may only use designated visitor parking stalls, on a first-come, first-served basis. If an owner needs visitor parking for more than 72 hours, the Condominium Manager must be notified. (Bylaw 62(c)(v))

2. Noise and Disturbances

  • Noise that is a nuisance or unreasonably interferes with the use and enjoyment of a Unit or Common Property is prohibited. (Bylaw 62(b)(vi))

3. Damage to Common Property

  • Owners are responsible for any maintenance, repair, or replacement made necessary by damage caused by themselves or their guests. (Bylaw 43(d))
  • Installation of security lights, doorbell cameras, motion sensors, or similar devices on communal property (bannisters, pillars, exteriors, etc.) requires prior Board approval.

4. Garbage

  • Residents must use designated waste/recycling areas and follow the City of Edmonton's waste management guidelines.
  • In accordance with the City of Edmonton Waste Services Bylaw, no person may interfere with, disturb, or remove the contents of a waste container.
  • Large items (furniture) or hazardous materials must be disposed of off-site at the owner's expense — not left in common areas.

5. Balcony and Patio Upkeep

  • Balconies and patios must be kept clean and free of clutter, debris, garbage, or items that pose a safety hazard or nuisance to other residents.
  • Residents must not hang laundry or other items in a manner that detracts from the building's aesthetic appearance. (Bylaw 62(b)(viii))

6. Window Coverings

  • Window coverings must be installed and maintained to preserve the uniform appearance of the building's exterior.
  • Reflective coverings that could disrupt the privacy of neighbouring units are not permitted.
  • Flags, bed sheets, aluminum foil, or other materials not intended as window coverings are not permitted when visible from outside.

7. Holiday Lights and Decorations

  • Holiday decorative lights/decorations may be displayed from November 1st; they must be removed no later than March 1st, and turned off by February 1st.
  • Other seasonal/occasion decorations are permitted within 3 weeks before and must be removed within 3 weeks after the occasion.
  • As a courtesy to neighbours, please turn lights off by 11 p.m.

8. Air Conditioning Units

  • Installation requires Board approval and must comply with building codes and regulations.
  • Regular maintenance and servicing — including all costs and repairs — is the owner's responsibility.
  • Installation must be performed by a licensed contractor carrying WCB and General Liability Insurance of at least $1 million.

9. Pets

  • Only domesticated pets (birds, fish, small animals, etc.) are permitted. Exotic, unusual, unknown, or venomous pets are prohibited; exceptions require Board approval after risk review. (Bylaw 62(b)(ii))
  • Dogs must not exceed 16 inches at the shoulder when fully grown, except certified service dogs approved by the Board. (Bylaw 62(b)(ii))
  • Owners must clean up after their pets and ensure pets don't disturb residents or damage property. Pets must be hand-leashed at all times on common property. (Bylaw 62(b)(ii))
  • Damage to common property is charged back to the pet's owner.

10. Insurance

Owners must maintain condominium insurance. Discuss the following with your broker (this list is not exhaustive):

  • Personal Property — recommended $50,000 for fire, water, windstorm, etc.
  • Improvements and Betterments — the Corporation typically does not cover improvements made by current or previous owners (unless required by the Bylaws).
  • Additional Living Expenses — coverage to live elsewhere if your unit becomes uninhabitable. Rule of thumb: enough for 18 months in the event of a total loss.
  • Loss Assessment Coverage — covers your share of any special assessment to make up a shortfall between damage and the Corporation's insurance limit (insured loss only).

11. Rental Units

  • All leased/rental units must comply with the Corporation's Bylaws and Policies. (Bylaw 51)
  • Owners renting out must provide tenant contact details and emergency contacts to the Condominium Manager.
  • Rental agreements must include a clause requiring tenants to abide by the Bylaws and Rules. The landlord is responsible for relaying relevant condo information to the tenant.
  • Tenants should have full replacement contents (personal property) coverage, additional living expense coverage, and general liability up to $1 million.

12. Renovations

  • Load-bearing walls and plumbing, mechanical, or electrical systems may not be altered without Board consent. (Bylaw 3(d))
  • All alterations/renovations (except minor cosmetic changes like wall painting) require Board approval before commencement; submit an accurate rendering of the proposed work.
  • Proper permits and licensed contractors must be used. An engineer's report may be required for extensive changes.
  • Unit owners are fully responsible for any damage to common property caused by alterations/renovations. All debris must be removed from the site by the owner — not placed in the household trash bins.

Reporting a Bylaw Violation

A clear, fair, and transparent process for residents to raise concerns and have them reviewed by the Board.

  1. Document the offense in writing. Note the date, time, location, nature of the issue, photographic evidence if available, and any witnesses present.
  2. Submit the written report. Send to the Condominium Manager — by email or physical mail — depending on their preference.
  3. Receive written acknowledgement. The Manager (or their designate) will confirm receipt in writing, including the date received and assurance the Board will review.
  4. Board review. The Manager presents the report at the next scheduled Board meeting (or a specially convened meeting if urgent). The Board reviews, gathers additional information if needed, and discusses possible actions.
  5. Decision making. The Board decides on the appropriate course of action: warning, fine, or other corrective measures under the Bylaws.
  6. Decision communicated. The Manager communicates the decision to the reporting resident and any relevant parties, including any sanctions imposed and timelines for compliance.
  7. Follow-up and monitoring. The Board may track compliance and address recurring issues.

Bylaws Reference

Summary of the 64 bylaws enacted by Condominium Corporation No. 1720499 on February 16, 2017 (Alberta Land Titles document 172058032). For the full legal text, request a copy from the Condominium Manager.

Bylaw 1Definitions

Defines key terms used throughout the bylaws: "Act" (the Alberta Condominium Property Act), "Board," "Common Property," "Corporation," "Owner," "Unit," "Unit Factor," and related terms.

Bylaw 2Application

The bylaws apply to all owners, occupants, tenants, visitors, invitees, and their guests.

Bylaw 3Duties of Owners

Owners must: (a) keep their Unit in good repair; (b) permit reasonable access to the Corporation for inspections/repairs; (c) not use the Unit in a way that interferes with others; (d) not alter load-bearing walls or plumbing, mechanical, or electrical systems without Board consent; (e) use and enjoy Common Property in accordance with the Bylaws and Rules; (f) pay all assessments and contributions when due; (g) notify the Corporation of changes of ownership/occupancy; (h) comply strictly with the Bylaws and Rules and ensure tenants, family, visitors, invitees, and other occupants do the same.

Bylaw 4Duties of the Corporation

The Corporation shall control, manage, and administer the Common Property; maintain and repair Common Property; comply with notices and orders from authorities; and enforce the Bylaws.

Bylaw 5Powers of the Corporation

The Corporation may, through its Board: (a) employ agents and servants; (b) make contracts; (c) acquire personal property; (d) borrow money; (e) invest funds; (f) commence/defend legal actions; (g) settle disputes; (h) levy assessments; (i) enter Units in emergencies; (j) make rules and regulations for the use, enjoyment, and safety of Common Property and enforce them, including action under Section 36 of the Act.

Bylaw 6The Board

The affairs of the Corporation shall be managed by a Board of Directors elected at general meetings.

Bylaw 7Number of Directors

The Board shall consist of a minimum and maximum number of directors as set out in the bylaws.

Bylaw 8Election of Directors

Directors are elected by ordinary resolution at the Annual General Meeting.

Bylaw 9Eligibility

Owners (and certain authorized representatives) are eligible for election to the Board.

Bylaw 10Term of Office

Directors hold office until the next AGM or until their successors are elected.

Bylaw 11Vacation of Office

A director's office becomes vacant on death, resignation, bankruptcy, ceasing to be eligible, or removal by special resolution.

Bylaw 12Removal of Directors

Directors may be removed by special resolution of the owners.

Bylaw 13Filling Vacancies

Vacancies may be filled by the remaining directors until the next general meeting.

Bylaw 14Officers

The Board shall elect from among its members a Chairperson, Vice-Chair, Secretary, and Treasurer (or combine these roles).

Bylaw 15Powers of the Board

The Board exercises all powers of the Corporation except those reserved to the owners.

Bylaw 16Board Meetings

Procedures for calling, holding, and conducting Board meetings.

Bylaw 17Quorum

The quorum for Board meetings is a majority of the directors then in office.

Bylaw 18Voting at Board Meetings

Each director has one vote; the Chair has a casting vote in the event of a tie.

Bylaw 19Resolutions in Writing

A resolution signed by all directors is as valid as one passed at a duly convened meeting.

Bylaw 20Indemnification of Directors

Directors are indemnified by the Corporation for acts done in good faith in the discharge of their duties.

Bylaw 21Remuneration

Directors may be reimbursed for reasonable expenses; remuneration requires approval at a general meeting.

Bylaw 22Delegation

The Board may delegate any of its powers to committees or to the Condominium Manager.

Bylaw 23Annual General Meeting

An AGM must be held each year within the period required by the Act.

Bylaw 24Special General Meetings

The Board may call special general meetings; owners holding 15% of unit factors may requisition one.

Bylaw 25Notice of Meetings

Written notice of general meetings must be given at least 14 days in advance, stating the date, time, place, and business to be transacted.

Bylaw 26Quorum at General Meetings

The quorum is owners (in person or by proxy) representing not less than 25% of unit factors.

Bylaw 27Chair of General Meetings

The Chair of the Board (or another director designated by the Board) chairs general meetings.

Bylaw 28Adjournment

A meeting may be adjourned if a quorum is not present within 30 minutes.

Bylaw 29Voting at General Meetings

Each owner has voting rights equal to their unit factor. Voting may be by show of hands or by poll.

Bylaw 30Joint Owners

Joint owners must agree on a single representative to vote; otherwise the vote is not counted.

Bylaw 31Proxies

An owner may vote by proxy. A proxy must be in writing, signed, and delivered to the Corporation before the meeting.

Bylaw 32Ordinary Resolutions

Pass by a simple majority of unit factors represented and voting.

Bylaw 33Special Resolutions

Require a 75% majority of unit factors and a 75% majority of owners present and voting.

Bylaw 34Unanimous Resolutions

Require the consent of all owners.

Bylaw 35Minutes

The Corporation shall keep minutes of all general meetings and Board meetings.

Bylaw 36Books and Records

The Corporation shall keep proper books of account and records; owners have inspection rights as set out in the Act.

Bylaw 37Auditor

An auditor may be appointed each year at the AGM.

Bylaw 38Financial Year

The financial year of the Corporation is set by the Board.

Bylaw 39Banking

The Board may open and operate bank accounts on behalf of the Corporation.

Bylaw 40Cheques

Cheques and negotiable instruments are signed by such officers/directors as the Board designates.

Bylaw 41Reserve Fund

The Corporation shall establish and maintain a reserve fund in accordance with the Act.

Bylaw 42Reserve Fund Study

A reserve fund study must be conducted in accordance with the Act and the regulations.

Bylaw 43Violations and Fines

Where an owner, tenant, visitor, or invitee breaches any Bylaw, Rule, or Regulation, the Corporation may: (a) impose a monetary penalty and charge interest on unpaid amounts; (b) issue warnings and notices of violation; (c) recover from the offending owner all costs of enforcement, including legal fees on a solicitor-and-client basis; (d) charge back the cost of repair/replacement of any damage caused, plus a maximum fine of $250 per offense. All amounts owing become part of the contribution levied against the owner's unit.

Bylaw 44Phasing

Provisions relating to the addition of phases to the Condominium Plan, if applicable.

Bylaw 45Damage and Destruction

Procedures following substantial damage or destruction of the buildings, including the application of insurance proceeds and any required owner approvals.

Bylaw 46Insurance

The Corporation shall maintain insurance against fire and other perils on all buildings and improvements, comprehensive general liability of not less than $2,000,000, directors' and officers' liability insurance, and any other insurance required by the Act or considered prudent. The Corporation's insurance does not cover owners' personal property, improvements/betterments inside units, or owners' personal liability — owners must carry their own coverage.

Bylaw 47Common Expenses and Assessments

The Board shall prepare an annual budget and levy contributions on each unit in proportion to its unit factor. Contributions are payable on the date(s) determined by the Board (typically monthly). Owners are liable to pay all contributions and assessments levied against their units.

Bylaw 48Special Assessments

The Board may levy special assessments to meet expenses not covered by the annual budget.

Bylaw 49Default in Payment

Where an owner is in default of any contribution or assessment, the Corporation may charge interest, register a caveat on title, and take legal action to recover amounts owing (including solicitor-and-client costs).

Bylaw 50Estoppel Certificate

The Corporation will provide an estoppel certificate (status certificate) in accordance with the Act on request, setting out outstanding amounts owing on a unit.

Bylaw 51Leasing of Units

Owners who lease their unit must: notify the Corporation in writing of the tenancy and provide tenant contact information; ensure the lease incorporates these Bylaws and Rules; and remain responsible for their tenant's compliance. Owners may be charged back for any fines or costs arising from tenant breaches.

Bylaw 52Severability

If any provision of these Bylaws is held invalid, the remainder shall continue in full force.

Bylaw 53Notices

Notices may be given in writing and delivered personally, by mail, by fax, or by email to the address most recently provided to the Corporation.

Bylaw 54Service on the Corporation

Notices to the Corporation must be delivered to its registered address.

Bylaw 55Waiver

A failure to enforce any Bylaw on one occasion does not constitute a waiver of the right to enforce it on any future occasion.

Bylaw 56Conflict

Where these Bylaws conflict with the Act, the Act prevails.

Bylaw 57Headings

Headings are for convenience only and do not affect interpretation.

Bylaw 58Gender and Number

Words importing the singular include the plural and vice versa; words importing a gender include all genders.

Bylaw 59Common Seal

The common seal of the Corporation is kept in the custody of the Secretary and is affixed under Board authority.

Bylaw 60Execution of Documents

Documents executed by the Corporation are signed by two officers or in such other manner as the Board directs.

Bylaw 61Rules

The Board may make and amend rules consistent with the Bylaws regarding the use, enjoyment, and safety of the Common Property, and such rules are binding on all owners and occupants.

Bylaw 62Use and Occupancy Restrictions

Owners and occupants must comply with the following restrictions on use of Units and Common Property:

  • (a) Each Unit shall be used as a private single-family dwelling and for no other purpose; no business or commercial use is permitted.
  • (b)(i) No occupant shall do anything in a Unit or on Common Property which is illegal, dangerous, or which would increase insurance premiums.
  • (b)(ii) Pets: Only domesticated pets permitted; dogs must be ≤ 16 inches at the shoulder when fully grown (except certified service dogs); pets must be hand-leashed on Common Property; owners are responsible for cleanup and any damage.
  • (b)(iii) Maximum of six (6) occupants per Unit.
  • (b)(iv) No noxious, offensive, or illegal activity is permitted.
  • (b)(v) Owners shall not store flammable or hazardous materials in their Unit or on Common Property.
  • (b)(vi) Noise: No noise that is a nuisance or that unreasonably interferes with another resident's enjoyment is permitted at any time.
  • (b)(vii) No signs, posters, or advertising visible from outside a Unit without Board consent.
  • (b)(viii) Laundry: No laundry or other items shall be hung where visible from outside.
  • (b)(ix) Window coverings must be of a uniform appearance; no flags, sheets, or improvised coverings visible from outside.
  • (b)(x) No alteration of the exterior of a Unit (including doors, windows, eavestroughs, paint) without Board consent.
  • (b)(xi) No structures (sheds, fences, antennae, satellite dishes) may be erected on Common Property without Board consent.
  • (b)(xii) No barbecues using solid fuel (charcoal, wood) on balconies; only propane or electric appliances permitted, in accordance with applicable codes.
  • (b)(xiii) Garbage and recycling must be placed in designated areas only.
  • (b)(xiv) No littering or accumulation of debris on Common Property.
  • (b)(xv) Children must be supervised on Common Property.
  • (b)(xvi) Smoking restrictions as set out by the Board.
  • (b)(xvii) No use of the Common Property in a way that interferes with others' enjoyment.
  • (b)(xviii) Owners must comply with all municipal, provincial, and federal laws.
  • (b)(xix) Construction/renovation work is permitted only during weekday business hours (typically 8:30 a.m. – 6:00 p.m.); no work before 8:30 a.m., after 6:00 p.m., or on weekends/statutory holidays without Board consent.
  • (b)(xx)–(xxvii) Additional restrictions including landscape modifications, exterior storage, vehicle repair, etc. — Board consent required.
  • (c) Parking:
    • (c)(i) Vehicles must be parked within owner's designated parking space, inside painted lines; no parking behind stalls or in front of garages.
    • (c)(ii) Parking units to be used for vehicles only — no recreational use of parking areas/roadways.
    • (c)(iii) Vehicles must be licensed, insured, and operational; no derelict or commercial vehicles.
    • (c)(iv) No parking on Common Property, in fire lanes, or in undesignated areas; offending vehicles may be towed at the owner's expense.
    • (c)(v) Visitor parking stalls are for visitors only, first-come first-served; owners using them for > 72 hours must notify the Manager.
    • (c)(vi) Vehicle repair (other than minor emergency repair) is not permitted on the property.

Bylaw 63Private Fire Hydrants

Private fire hydrants on the property shall be inspected, tested, and maintained in accordance with the Alberta Fire Code 2006 and NFPA 25 (Standard for the Inspection, Testing, and Maintenance of Water-Based Fire Protection Systems). The Corporation is responsible for ensuring these requirements are met.

Bylaw 64Amendment of Bylaws

These Bylaws may be amended, repealed, or replaced only by special resolution (75% of unit factors and 75% of owners present and voting). Amendments take effect once registered at the Alberta Land Titles Office.
